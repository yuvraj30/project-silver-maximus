Case question 3 of 5

Where could AI and automation improve patient access and lower cost, which uses should Bellwood fund within the $35M limit, and what safeguards would protect clinical quality and patient trust?

B · ActFix cost and access inside the network Bellwood already owns. See the pyramid

Our answer

Fund workflow first. Scale AI only where benefit and safety are demonstrated.

Approve $5.0M for scheduling and referrals now ($1.5M a year to run) and $1.5M for shared data and assurance ($0.4M a year). Release $2.0M for billing ($0.5M a year) and $0.3M for note-taking setup ($0.6M a year for 200 clinicians) at Gate 1. That is $8.8M one-time and $3.0M a year of recurring cost, within the $32.8M approve-now ceiling, which itself sits within the $35M cap. Savings count only as real expense removal, and many gains need ordinary workflow automation rather than generative AI.

Fund first · $5.0M + $1.5M a year

Scheduling and referral tracking

Supports the 26% without booking within 60 days and the 26.2% of attended first visits without a recorded report sent in that window. These are different stage-specific measures. A missing report is not a lost attended visit; do not add the rates as lost patients.

Rules / assisted booking→Staff handle exceptions→Clinicians set urgency rules

Pilot locally using matched cohorts. Case implementation range is $4–6M; the selected $5.0M amount is proposed. Base value: 25% of a $30M administrative sub-pool ($7.5M), released over three tiers; pilot gates the rollout.

Gate 1 release · $2.0M + $0.5M a year

Billing and coding automation

Part of the $60M administrative activity pool: 20% of a $30M sub-pool ($6.0M Base value), released over two tiers. Supports billing clarity, a top need for other-specialty patients (54%). Revenue-integrity work (denials, CDI, underpayments) sits in the hospital program and is counted once there; billing automation counts only cost removal, so no AI line double counts it.

AI suggests codes→Certified coder reviews→Monthly upcoding audit

Validate against documentation and payer rules; monitor unsupported coding. No revenue uplift is booked without collectible net-revenue evidence.

Gate 1 release · $0.3M + $0.6M a year

AI note-taking

The case pilot reduced documentation time about 25%. Keep that local result and test generalization by specialty. External trials show variable effects; they do not justify replacing the case result with a universal 10% assumption. The only modelled benefit is about 1,000 bookable orthopedic slots, set out on Q2 assumption; no separate scribe productivity revenue is counted.

AI drafts note→Clinician edits and signs→Sample audit

$3,000 per clinician a year case; 200 users is a planning assumption assumption. Shared data, privacy and AI assurance: $1.5M plus $0.4M a year.

Do not fund nowClinical triage or diagnosis AI.The mandate includes AI broadly. These higher-consequence uses are deferred because the case supplies no clinical validation and they are unnecessary for the first operating improvements.

Every AI use keeps a human sign-off, a fair-access check and a phone path

  • Human in the loop: clinicians sign every AI note; coders validate assisted coding and review exceptions; staff can override any booking.
  • Accuracy: validate before launch and use risk-based ongoing audits for omissions, unsupported content, negation and medication errors. Serious incidents require immediate review.
  • Fair access: never give worse slots to patients flagged as likely no-shows. Report waits by language, age, payer and location.
  • Trust: tell patients how the tool is used, obtain and document applicable consent, offer an accessible alternative and keep a phone path.
  • Staff: stabilize safe coverage. Redeployment creates capacity, not cash savings; cash benefits require actual expense reduction net of replacement costs.
  • Governance: use NIST Govern/Map/Measure/Manage; assign clinical stop authority. Validate applicable recording, consent, privacy and vendor requirements with counsel; no state-law conclusion is asserted.

Proposed operating rules, not certified clinical thresholds. The quality council sets validated monitoring tolerances and sample requirements. Retain assisted access, approved data environments, audit logs, vendor agreements and tested rollback.
